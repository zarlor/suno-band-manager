The last ferry leaves at eleven forty and I am always on it, coat collar up, watching the city fold itself into a thin gold line behind the wake, and the deckhand who never speaks coils the rope the same way every night like a prayer he learned before he learned his name, and I think about my father who crossed this same water every morning for thirty one years with a thermos and a newspaper he never read, who told me once that the trick to living on an island is to love the leaving as much as the coming home, and I did not understand him then, but tonight the engine shudders under my feet and the gulls have gone quiet and the far shore is only a rumor of lights, and I finally understand that he was never talking about the ferry at all.
